## Positioning claim

JoyJoin's personality assessment is built on the Five-Factor lineage that replaced
typological systems like MBTI in academic psychology (McCrae & Costa, 1989;
Pittenger, 2005). Its adaptive engine uses computerized adaptive testing methods
validated for personality measurement (Waller & Reise, 1989; Holden & Bernstein,
2000). Its matching model applies two decades of team-composition meta-analytic
evidence showing that group-level personality configuration predicts collective
outcomes (Bell, 2007; Barrick et al., 1998), and the similarity-attraction
literature showing trait resemblance drives interpersonal affinity (Montoya et
al., 2008; Selfhout et al., 2010).

## Why continuous traits, not types

Typological systems (MBTI's 16 types) force continuous variation into dichotomies,
discarding exactly the information needed to compare individuals. Peer-reviewed
reanalysis showed MBTI scales are simply noisy Big Five measures whose types add
no explanatory power, and that the instrument has poor test-retest stability and
no demonstrated outcome validity.

JoyJoin's ACOEXP model (Affinity, Conscientiousness, Emotional Stability,
Openness, Extraversion, Positivity; each scored 0–100) is a social-behavior
remix of the Big Five lineage, keeping scores continuous so that matching
remains a geometry problem.

## Why trait composition predicts group outcomes

Two decades of organizational psychology meta-analysis show that the
personality composition of a group — not just individual member quality —
predicts how that group performs and coheres.

Applied Psychology. doi:10.1037/0021-9010.92.3.595 | Team-level Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, and Emotional Stability predict team performance, with stronger effects in field settings | 1142 |

JoyJoin's pool matching operates at exactly this level: group composition over
six continuous trait dimensions, with floor effects (no member below a
stability threshold) and variance shaping (a group needs an energy spark, not
six clones) — a direct application of the composition findings above to
first-meeting social tables.

Effects are real but moderate (r ≈ .2–.3), so JoyJoin pairs similarity on
warmth dimensions with complementarity on energy dimensions (one high-Positivity
spark in a calm group), encoded in the archetype chemistry matrix.

## Why adaptive measurement is rigorous, not a shortcut

The V4 assessment (8–16 questions drawn adaptively from a 60+ item bank until
confidence and coverage thresholds are met) is standard psychometric practice,
not a shortened quiz.

## Measurement backbone

- **IPIP** (International Personality Item Pool, ipip.ori.org): 3,320
  public-domain items, free for any use including commercial, with existing
  Chinese translations. This is the instrument for our convergent-validity
  study.

## Pre-launch validation program (remote-only)

1. **Convergent validity** — 300–500 remote participants take V4 + IPIP-NEO
   Big Five; target r > 0.6 on mapped factors.
2. **Remote vibe simulation** — 15-min video mini-tables of 4–6 strangers;
   regress trait-based group composition against post-session 同频 /
   would-meet-again ratings.
3. **Test-retest** — same panel re-tested at 4 weeks for stability.
4. **Perceived accuracy A/B** — narrative variants tested on the panel
   (answer-citing vs generic; contrastive runner-up section vs not).

## Known limitations (state these before critics do)

- ACOEXP is internally validated (persona simulation suite, matcher isolation
  tests) but not yet externally validated against a benchmark instrument;
  item 1 above closes this.
- Similarity-attraction effects are moderate; we claim "measurably improves
  the odds of a compatible table," never "guarantees chemistry."
- Narrative self-recognition partly reflects the Barnum effect (Forer, 1949);
  we treat it as a presentation concern, not a validity claim.
